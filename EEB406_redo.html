Since then, I gained more experience with R, which I believe helped me in 2 ways. For one, I know more functions and how to do things. Additionally, I have a better idea of what I can do with R. I previously had some experience with python (from CSC108H1 (Intro to Computer Programming) back in early 2nd year), but I knew so little about R it didn’t even occur to me that I could write for loops in R.

My current approach (with commentary)

- I think I like to easily modify my inputs to get different outputs.

For example, while I did my project on chloride and nitrate (and since this is based on something I have already submitted, it’s not like this will change), I still wrote it as if I may change it as I think this is how I would write it these days. However, I would still assume that the underlying dataset will is still the same one and wrote accordingly.

- I like to write the modifiable outputs at the top.

As you can see, all the inputs is modified before the for loop, with stops to check that the input is correct. I do this partly out of habbit, and because it makes inputs easier to find and modify.

- I like for loops.

I am aware that there are neater ways of writing this (expand.grid (which should save at least 2 for loops) and purrr::map come to mind), but I didn’t do that.

One reason is because I am less familiar with these methods (I learned about for loops in CSC108). Another would be how I approach coding (and I suspect writing in general). I tend to write as I go, letting structure arise as I write. In terms of coding, I get a rough idea of the steps to complete, and figure out my approach as I code. This lends itself to a lot of for loops, as one needs simply to add another loop rather than figuring out how to re-structure what they have written.
